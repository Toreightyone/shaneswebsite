*Originally submitted to the Toronto Star on June 10, 2026*

This past weekend from Ossington Avenue to Lansdowne Avenue on Dundas Street was an absolute party. It was so much fun walking from end to end and seeing vendors from dozens of cultures and ethnicities: jewellery, handcrafts, desserts, food, buskers, attempts at karaoke, and micro-raves at DJs' booths. However, I went on Sunday, hours after demand clearly exceeded capacity. Videos of severe crowding and shoving demonstrate that the people yearn for more opportunities to just be outside and hang out.

We spend winter trapped inside, and once summer comes, the first opportunity to take to the streets brings everyone, and I mean everyone, out. Do West Fest was just this year's first victim; a victim of its own success perhaps. Vendors got sales; businesses saw more foot traffic; and attendees, overall, enjoyed themselves while participating in, contributing to, and growing the local economy. The city's philosophy around these types of open streets constrains these benefits to one weekend a year for maybe a few blocks when the demand is clearly there for closing larger sections of roadway, reoccurring street festivals, and even creating more permanent fixtures.

Two and a half years ago, [I wrote about dangerous conditions at Union Station for New Year's Eve](https://www.thestar.com/opinion/contributors/new-years-eve-transit-fiasco-may-result-in-fatalities-next-year/article_e1230fa8-a995-11ee-a113-8f95dc791dab.html) with thousands overwhelming both the TTC and GO stations. My suggestion was a street festival that would spread people out throughout the downtown and towards the other ten subway stations, while giving people a reason to interact with our urban space beyond just seeing the fireworks and going home. Unfortunately, the status quo is that we cannot possibly be bold in creating pedestrian spaces despite it objectively being a net positive from numerous different aspects like the economy, health, culture, social connection, and yes, [traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braess%27s_paradox).

It is such a fight to create these types of spaces. Pedestrian Sundays at Kensington Market, a measly one Sunday per month, is such a pleasant experience to shop without having to risk your life walking into the road because the sidewalk is clogged, but it was shuttered in May 2025 only to return earlier this June (and hopefully for the rest of the summer). The pedestrianization pilot project in the Church and Wellesley Neighbourhood, desperately needed given the sheer volume of people nightly, [was shortened considerably from Rodney Chan's original vision](https://x.com/_ChanFace/status/1901308090517004332), had ample pushback about hypotheticals, and thankfully passed City Council albeit with an abort button.

Meanwhile, I look to other cities' successes with open streets and how good it has been for businesses while changing planning ontology to prioritise enjoyment over strictly movement: Victoria, Montreal, Calgary(!), and Kitchener-Waterloo just to name a few in Canada. The closure of Pike Place Market to cars in Seattle saw [6.5% more sales for tenants as compared to 2024 (8.3% when comparing May-September), 145,000 additional visits year-over-year from 127,000 additional visitors, and users reported preferring the vehicle restrictions over not.](https://pikeplacemarket.community.highbond.com/document/65a63808-919b-4dea-a0f8-4a253aff3852/) People want this, and they want more of this. I liken it to a baby refusing to eat their vegetables: it might be a tough sell initially, but it is demonstrably good.

How would I improve Do West Fest for next year? By expanding it eastward from Ossington as far as Spadina or Yonge or Broadview; have it be a proper celebration. We could have the economic and social benefits of the Taste of Little Italy, Taste of the Danforth, or the Cabbagetown Festival among others along much larger swaths of roadway with more frequency to handle those massive crowds we saw and provide a long-term support for businesses and BIAs. Bureaucracy should be streamlined and funding should be there so this can happen. We can be a city of fun, or a city that spends more effort finding excuses not to be.
