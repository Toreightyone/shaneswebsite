*Originally submitted to the Toronto Star on July 25, 2023*

[On Monday evening, the trailing car of a train on the Scarborough RT decoupled and then derailed](https://www.thestar.com/news/gta/ttc-says-line-3-service-may-not-resume-for-several-days-after-scarborough-train-derailment/article_96226692-fb9c-5afa-ad51-3c84598fe099.html#:~:text=On%20Tuesday%2C%20the%20TTC%20said%20there%20is%20still,take%20%E2%80%9Cseveral%20days%20at%20minimum%E2%80%9D%20to%20restore%20service.), leading to five passengers being sent to hospital. While we await a review determining the exact cause for the car's derailment, political infighting for decades has left the aging SRT vulnerable to incidents like these.

Built in 1985, the line had an expected lifespan of thirty years, bringing us to 2015. According to a 2016 report brought forward by a [CTV News request under the Freedom of Information Act in 2021](https://toronto.ctvnews.ca/duct-tape-used-as-a-sealant-toronto-councillor-pushes-for-full-release-of-scarborough-rt-repair-report-1.5346904), SRT cars faced major issues dealing with the wear and tear of brakes, doors, wiring, and communication equipment. Most parts were no longer in production and had to be produced in-house, while duct tape was being used in routine maintenance.

A light rail line was proposed to replace the SRT as a part of David Miller's Transit City plan in 2007, alongside similar lines along [Jane, Finch, Sheppard East, Eglinton, and Don Mills](https://www.thestar.com/opinion/good-move-on-transit/article_de0db789-c903-5650-8516-6ccb2bacd37b.html). The conversion of the SRT to light rail with an extension to Malvern Town Centre was fully funded to the tune of [\$1.4 billion by Dalton McGuinty's Liberal government in April 2009](https://news.ontario.ca/en/backgrounder/5234/province-moving-transit-projects-forward) and construction was slated to begin in 2010 with an approximate completion date of 2015.

The province's April 2010 budget resulted in the slashing of \$4 billion from Metrolinx's budget and [thus funding was cut for all Transit City projects](https://nationalpost.com/posted-toronto/fight-begins-to-save-transit-city) except for the in-progress Sheppard East LRT. The election of Rob Ford in October 2010 put the nail in the coffin for the entire plan as he proclaimed that "the people want subways," and buried projects including the SRT replacement. Ford campaigned on extending the Bloor-Danforth subway from Kennedy to Scarborough Town Centre, of which the details and number of stops were debated, planned, funded, scrapped, rinse and repeat. Debates raged between the mayor, council, and the provincial and federal governments for almost a decade until [work on the Line 2 extension began in 2021](https://toronto.ctvnews.ca/ontario-breaks-ground-on-scarborough-subway-extension-1.5482761) and [tunnelling began earlier this year](https://toronto.citynews.ca/2023/01/17/scarborough-subway-extension-tunnelling/).

2030 is the expected year the SRT's replacement will be completed, twenty-three years after Miller's proposed replacement, but there is little hope that date will stick given the city/province's history with over-budget and delayed projects.

The biggest loser has been patient Scarborough commuters who have been told time and time again that relief is coming. Almost a quarter of a lifetime has been spent waiting for a plan to replace their only meaningful rapid transit line. The decommissioning date has been known since 1985, forty-five years, and yet the proverbial football has been punted between governments past, subject to significant major change by changes in leadership. The TTC has done absolutely amazing work, given the scarce resources they are given, to extend the lifespan of the line as much as possible to allow for governments to build something, anything. They reached their absolute limit for what is possible and gave a November 2023 end date, and yet five months until then, contingency plans for the seven years between decommissioning and opening of the subway extension have yet to be decided and funded by levels of government.

The SRT's rollingstock and infrastructure has been pushed as far as it could while bureaucratic folly plays its course, and it is no surprise that Monday's derailment could take out the line early. An incident like this was inevitable and blame can be spread across the past two decades, across all levels of government, and across the political spectrum for failing to agree on a solution despite a due date far into the future.

There is some hope as construction on transit projects has either started, or is nearing completion city-wide, but Scarborough commuters will imminently face a significant gap in their already arduous commute. It is imperative that current governments at all levels agree and fund one solution at a time, starting with complete bus priority for shuttle buses, followed by replacing the right-of-way with a busway that has the support of Mayor Olivia Chow, and continuing with meaningful improvement to Scarborough's transit map to finally give the much-needed support residents have been demanding for years.

_Shane Gates is an undergraduate student at the University of Toronto majoring in urban planning._
