*Originally submitted to the Toronto Star on July 2, 2026*

If you told me a few months ago the CBC would join the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) as an equal member to the BBC, RAI, and France Télévisions, and Canada would participate in the 2027 Eurovision Song Contest in Bulgaria, I would have ordered you an MRI stat.

Like Prime Minister Mark Carney, I have also been watching the show for quite some time: from the individual countries' selection contests like Sweden's _Melodifestivalen_, Finland's _Uuden Musiikin Kilpailu_, Albania's _Festivali i Këngës,_ or Italy's _Festival di San Remo_; to the two semifinals; to the grand final. A constant thought of mine had always been "what if Canada joined?". We had done so in the 1987 and 1989 editions of Eurovision Young Dancers and several of our singers participated for other countries; notably Céline Dion with her winning song "_Ne Partez Pas Sans Moi_" for Switzerland in 1988 and more recently [Montreal-born La Zarra](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/montreal/la-zarra-eurovision-1.6823468) and her song "_Évidemment_" for France in 2023. The stipulations of sending our own entry under the red maple leaf is exactly what Canada needs right now on multiple fronts: geopolitically, culturally, and to unify our country.

The roughly-half of countries that choose their entry via a sort of mini-Eurovision showcases up-and-coming artists, established artists, and the musical nuances of different ethnic groups. Even when the public broadcaster chooses the song internally, it still produces that rally-around-the-flag effect that we could really use right about now. It is a new opportunity to cheer on Canada beyond sports, as every year will present a new opportunity to stand behind the country, the artist representing us, and their song, while bonding with our European friends at a time when we need to wane influence from the United States.

Countries' national selections often see sizeable percentages of their countries' populations watching to vote on which song best represents them. Our entry could be chosen democratically in the same way. Maybe a song from each of the thirteen provinces and territories could compete to represent Canada. We would get a chance to see genres, instruments, dances, and cultures from coast to coast to coast that we may have never been exposed to before, especially ones Indigenous or regional: Inuit throat singing, Newfoundlander jigs, Albertan country, or Québécois indie. It is a chance to unite the country through friendly competition.

It is also the outlet that our music industry needs to showcase our talent domestically and globally. Perhaps we could even win and then host the contest (good for tourism). The Discord server I am in's prediction contest had 450 guesses for 2026, and not one had Bulgaria winning; it was a complete shock (to the detriment of the Finnish half of my family). Win or not, the artists participating in a hypothetical selection contest would get a streaming boost from Canadians and self-titled Eurofans, while Canada's chosen artist would be seen by over 150,000,000 viewers. We may end up producing the next homegrown viral TikTok hit.

That being said, it is quite odd the most bureaucratic entity on the planet welcomed a North American country with open arms into the quintessential European event. The motive becomes obvious when you see [that viewership declined between 2025 and 2026](https://eurovoix.com/2026/06/05/eurovision-2026-audience-down-compared-to-2025/), [likely attributed to the boycott by Spain, Iceland, Ireland, Slovenia, and the Netherlands due to the continued inclusion of Israel's KAN](https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/iceland-eurovision-boycott-9.7010516). The 35 participants in 2026 represented the lowest number since 2003. The EBU is clearly desperate for both our money as a new participation fee-paying country and our untapped viewership to prop up the show. Although a positive overall, it is completely fair to ask if we are entering an arena that is both genuine and aligns with Canadian values of human rights and peace.
